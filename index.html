[Prototype] Infliximab GHG Emissions Calculator

This tool estimates greenhouse gas emissions (GHG emissions) associated with 8-week subcutaneous (s.c.) and intravenous (i.v.) infliximab treatment for inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), reported in kg CO2-eq. The model equations are derived from a life-cycle assessment (LCA) conducted in two Dutch university hospitals.

Important: estimates based on assumptions Results are indicative estimates, not exact measurements. They depend on model assumptions, emission factors, the values entered, and the treatment and logistical pathways represented in the model. Actual emissions may differ between patients, healthcare settings, transport choices, and supply arrangements. Results should be interpreted in the context of these assumptions and the underlying LCA.
Model application in additional hospitals In addition to the original LCA setting, the model has been applied to data from two other Dutch hospitals: one academic hospital and one non-academic hospital.

Model and formulas

The following equations calculate GHG emissions in kg CO2-eq for 8-week treatment with s.c. and i.v. infliximab. These equations are used in the GHG emissions model and the open-access online calculation tool. The model estimates total GHG emissions by combining emissions associated with the relevant treatment and logistical components.

Subcutaneous GHG emissions

s.c. = (0.42 × dose_s.c. + 0.96) / interval_s.c. + supply_emissions

Supply emissions depend on the selected pathway:

Efficient pick-up: 0 Delivery: 4.15 × 10⁻⁶ × distance Inefficient pick-up: (8 / interval_s.c. / n_dispensed) × (2 × distance × EFtype)

Here, s.c. is the estimated GHG emissions of 8-week s.c. infliximab treatment; dose_s.c. is the s.c. dose per administration in mg; interval_s.c. is the number of weeks between administrations; distance is the one-way distance from hospital to home; EFtype is the emission factor for the selected transport type; and n_dispensed is the number of s.c. pens dispensed per delivery or pick-up.

The three supply pathways represent efficient pick-up (collection alongside a planned hospital or pharmacy visit), home delivery, and inefficient pick-up (a separate trip made specifically to collect the pens).

Intravenous GHG emissions

i.v. = (0.56 × dose_i.v. × weight + 16 × EFtype × distance + 11.25) / interval_i.v.

Here, i.v. is the estimated GHG emissions of 8-week i.v. infliximab treatment; dose_i.v. is the i.v. dose in mg/kg; weight is patient weight in kg; interval_i.v. is the number of weeks between administrations; distance is the one-way distance from hospital to home; and EFtype is the emission factor for the selected patient transport type.

The equations are implemented as supplied. Distance is not automatically doubled in the i.v. equation; the factor of 2 appears explicitly only in the inefficient s.c. pick-up equation.
